# MEMORY.md

## What This File Is

- `MEMORY.md` is the curated long-term memory for stable preferences, decisions, project locations, and operating rules.
- Do not use `MEMORY.md` as a raw activity log.
- Put daily execution logs, transient failures, and one-off status updates in `memory/YYYY-MM-DD.md`.
- Promote a daily note into `MEMORY.md` only when it changes future behavior.
- Do not rely on `memory/YYYY-MM-DD.md` for stable behavior rules; daily notes may be skipped, summarized, archived, or cleaned. Any rule that should survive future sessions must be promoted into `MEMORY.md` and, when operationally important, mirrored into `SOUL.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `HEARTBEAT.md`, or the relevant skill.

## Knowledge Publishing Mission

- OpenClaw / IsaacBot should become a knowledge publishing system for high-quality source-backed topics across categories.
- 小舟 scope is technical, AI, backend/software, and engineering-company/operator sources. Do not intentionally use other topic types as filler.
- Preferred source types: primary engineering blogs, official docs/release notes, research posts, named researcher/team blogs, authoritative project updates, and high-signal technical videos with enough substance to explain.
- Source-intro/video selection should stay with engineering/AI/backend/software/operator sources.
- Automatic video topic selection may still prioritize strong technical AI/backend topics when they are the best candidates, but this is a preference/ranking signal, not a 小舟 scope restriction.
- AI remains an important core lane, but the selection tradeoff is source diversity, not an AI cap. Before choosing an automatic topic, inspect recent published `source.json` artifacts and apply a soft penalty when the same domain, registry source, or source family has appeared repeatedly; prefer a similarly strong source from a different original site or evidence type unless the repeated source is urgent, unusually deep, or clearly best.
- AI topic selection must include temporal judgment. Treat 30 days as the freshness baseline for automatic AI topics. Older AI sources are allowed only when the content is durable, historical, conceptual, architectural, or retrospective, and the artifact must record why it is still valid now. Time-sensitive AI claims such as model releases, API behavior, pricing, benchmarks, evals, coding-agent performance, inference cost/latency, tooling, and product claims should be rejected or refreshed with newer primary evidence when older than about 30 days.
- Comprehensive engineering support means rotating strong technical coverage across AI engineering, backend/API architecture, databases/data systems, distributed systems, streaming/messaging, cloud/infra/SRE/observability, security/privacy/compliance, runtimes/languages/frameworks, developer tooling/CI/CD/testing/coding agents, performance/cost/capacity, incident/operations, and product/operator collaboration that changes engineering judgment.
- For the `technical` lane, the default public purpose is helping viewers understand the selected original source, not targeting a role or selling a use case. Backend specialists, engineering learners, and cross-domain readers are all acceptable audiences, but public openings should not list PMs, business owners, teachers, or AI-software participants as the promise. A technical candidate should be accepted when it has a strong original source and can make the source's object, terms, mechanism, result, limits, and context easier to understand.
- Engineering and operator interviews are technical-lane candidates when they explain a concrete mechanism, tradeoff, operating model, or source-backed experience about product strategy, company structure, AI adoption, engineering/product collaboration, or software-building work. The public explanation should start from who is speaking and what question the source answers, not from "what every role should do next." Practical implications are secondary and must stay source-supported.
- Keep source pools and artifact domains organized for operational clarity, but do not use source-pool separation as a public scope blocker.
- Technical video topic priority: AI-related topics can be weighted above other technical categories when they have source quality, temporal relevance, and enough substance to explain clearly. If the candidate pool contains a high-quality, non-duplicate, verifiable AI-related source with concrete source content, no recent source-concentration problem, and no unresolved stale-risk, choose it as the main technical topic before backend or general technical topics. Non-AI technical topics fill when AI candidates are too thin, stale, duplicated, unverifiable, below the quality threshold, source-repetitive without exceptional value, or cannot be made understandable from the source.
- AI-related papers are priority topic sources only when their research question, method, result, and limitation can be explained clearly for first-time viewers. Prioritize papers on agents, coding/codegen, RAG/search/source attribution, eval/safety/reliability, inference cost/latency/serving, tool/browser/GUI use, long context/memory, multimodal systems, open models, post-training, or deployment workflows. Method/eval/artifacts are required evidence, not enough by themselves; narrow-domain or pure-theory papers are noise unless they can be introduced as a clear source-backed concept.
- AI leader/researcher/builder interviews are priority topic sources when they are AI-relevant and have enough transcript, show notes, chaptering, linked references, or concrete claims to explain. Use the interview episode URL as the main source, then turn the guest's mental model into mechanism/tradeoff/risk/impact, not a personality profile.
- Do not rely only on podcast/RSS pages for AI interviews. Important talks may appear only as YouTube channel/conference videos, such as Sequoia AI Ascent. Maintain channel-parser sources for frontier AI interview channels, and let influential AI builders/researchers with concrete claims about AGI timelines, world models, AI for science, drug discovery, safety/governance, reasoning/planning limits, or deployment impact surface as high-priority candidates.
- Hacker News is part of technical topic discovery via the `hn-daily-top-1500` flow. Use HN to detect recent hot AI/backend/infra/security/data/devtools topics, but treat it as discovery context rather than the main public source. For video selection, trace HN items back to the original article/repo/paper/release/advisory and require the same viewer-value, verification, dedupe, and non-marketing gates as other sources.
- Topic selection should continuously optimize using `SOURCE_REGISTRY.yml`, `SOURCE_POOL.md`, daily `reports/source-watch/*.jsonl`, source health, recent video `source.json` files, each video's `review/improvement-notes.md`, and safe public technical community signals. Raise or keep weights for sources that repeatedly produce deep review-passing videos; lower or disable sources that repeatedly produce thin, promotional, duplicate, or unverifiable candidates.

Write `review/ai-usage-improvement.md` for every run: source insight, affected workflow, recommendation (keep/change/test/backlog/no-op), next action, confidence/risk.
